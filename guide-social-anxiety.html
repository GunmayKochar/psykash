Managing Social Anxiety

Tools to navigate social situations with more ease, overcome the fear of judgement, and build genuine connections.

Why Does This Happen?

Humans are biologically wired to belong to a group. In ancient times, being rejected by the tribe meant you wouldn't survive. Therefore, caring about what others think is deeply ingrained in our DNA.

Social anxiety occurs when this natural desire for acceptance becomes an overwhelming fear of negative evaluation. Your brain begins to perceive normal social interactions—like speaking in a meeting or chatting at a party—as dangerous threats, triggering a profound fear of embarrassment.

Signs You Might Relate

  • I frequently replay social interactions in my head, focusing on what I did wrong.
  • I often cancel plans at the last minute because the thought of going is too stressful.
  • I worry excessively about appearing foolish, sweating, or blushing in front of others.
  • I rehearse what I am going to say before making a simple phone call.
  • I avoid making eye contact when talking to new people.

Practical Self-Help Guide

Here are five evidence-informed strategies to help you manage social situations.

Tip 1

Shift Your Focus Outward

In social situations, anxious people tend to focus internally (monitoring their heartbeat, wondering how they look). Shifting focus outward breaks this loop.

Try this today

Pay close attention to the other person's eye colour or the exact words they are saying, rather than your internal sensations.

Tip 2

Drop the Safety Behaviours

Safety behaviours (like checking your phone constantly, or staying near the exit) offer short-term relief but keep you trapped in the long run by confirming that the situation was "dangerous."

Try this today

Identify one safety behaviour you rely on, and actively choose not to use it during your next conversation.

Tip 3

Plan a Realistic Exit Strategy

Knowing you can leave if you need to reduces the feeling of being trapped, which ironically makes you more likely to stay.

Try this today

Before attending an event, decide on a polite excuse you can use to leave after 30 minutes. Once there, you may find you want to stay.

Tip 4

Test Your Assumptions

Social anxiety relies on the assumption that everyone is scrutinising you. The reality is that most people are completely focused on themselves.

Try this today

Next time you feel judged, gently remind yourself: "People are too busy worrying about their own lives to over-analyse mine."

Tip 5

Gradual Exposure

Avoidance fuels anxiety. Slowly exposing yourself to low-stakes social situations trains your brain that they are safe.

Try this today

Make brief eye contact and smile at one stranger today, like a barista or a cashier.

Today's Challenge

Send a quick text to a friend or colleague you haven't spoken to in a while, just to say hello.

"Care about what other people think and you will always be their prisoner."

When to Seek Professional Support

Self-help is a wonderful starting point, but it does not replace professional therapy. If your social fears persist and interfere with your daily life, work, or relationships, please reach out to a licensed professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is social anxiety the same as being introverted?

No. Introversion is a personality trait where you recharge by being alone. Social anxiety is a fear of judgement during social interactions. Introverts can be highly confident, and extroverts can have social anxiety.

Can I cure social anxiety completely?

While you may always have some baseline nervousness, evidence-based treatments like CBT can reduce social anxiety to a point where it no longer limits your life choices.

Why do I blush when I get anxious?

When adrenaline is released, your blood vessels dilate to improve blood flow. Blushing is simply a visible sign of this completely normal physiological response.

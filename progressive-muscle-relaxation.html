Progressive Muscle Relaxation

Release deep physical tension stored in the body.

What is this technique?

Progressive Muscle Relaxation (PMR) is a deep relaxation technique that involves systematically tensing and then completely releasing different muscle groups in the body, one by one.

When should I use it?

PMR is most effective when used preventatively or during recovery periods. It is highly recommended for insomnia, chronic tension headaches, jaw clenching, and generalized physical stress. It is best practiced in a quiet environment when you have 10-15 minutes.

Why does it work?

Anxiety often manifests as chronic, unnoticed physical tension. PMR works on the principle of the "relaxation pendulum": by deliberately creating maximum tension in a muscle, the muscle is forced to swing back into a deeper state of relaxation when released than it was originally in. It also trains your brain to recognize what true physical relaxation feels like.

Step-by-Step Instructions

1

Find a comfortable position.

Lie down flat on your back or sit in a supportive chair. Close your eyes and take three deep breaths.

2

Tense a specific muscle group.

Start with your feet and toes. Curl them tightly and hold the maximum tension for 5 seconds. Notice what the tension feels like.

3

Release completely.

Instantly and completely let go of the tension. Exhale as you release. Notice the contrast between the tightness and the new feeling of heaviness and relaxation. Rest for 10 seconds.

4

Move up the body.

Progress sequentially: lower legs, thighs, buttocks, stomach, chest, back, hands/arms, shoulders, neck, and finally the face and jaw. Tense for 5 seconds, release, rest for 10 seconds.

5

Full body scan.

Once you have reached your head, scan your body for any remaining pockets of tension. If you find one, repeat the tense-and-release cycle on that specific area.

Common Mistakes

  • Tensing muscles to the point of pain or cramping. You want tension, not injury.
  • Releasing the tension slowly. The release should be sudden and complete to trigger the relaxation response.
  • Holding your breath while tensing the muscles. Keep breathing normally.

Tips for getting the most benefit

  • Pay special attention to your shoulders and jaw, as these are the most common areas where adults store unconscious stress.
  • If you are short on time, you can do a "mini-PMR" by just tensing and releasing your shoulders, fists, and jaw.
  • Practice this daily before bed to dramatically improve sleep onset time.

Frequently Asked Questions

What if an area of my body is injured?

Skip any muscle groups that are injured, painful, or recovering from surgery. Focus on the surrounding healthy muscles instead.

Is it normal to feel a tingling sensation?

Yes, a warm, heavy, or slightly tingling sensation is completely normal and is a sign that the muscles are deeply relaxing and blood flow is increasing.

Can I listen to music while doing this?

Yes, soft, instrumental music without a driving beat can enhance the relaxation effect.

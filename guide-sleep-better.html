How to Sleep Better

A practical guide to sleep hygiene, building effective wind-down routines, and managing nighttime anxiety.

Why Does This Happen?

Sleep is not an on/off switch; it's a gradual process. When you struggle to sleep, it is rarely a problem with the act of sleeping itself. It is usually a problem with the transition from wakefulness to rest.

Modern life is intensely stimulating. If you spend your evening answering emails, watching gripping television, or scrolling through social media, your brain is flooded with cortisol and blue light. By the time your head hits the pillow, your nervous system is simply too wired to shut down.

Signs You Might Relate

  • I toss and turn for over an hour before finally falling asleep.
  • I wake up multiple times during the night and struggle to get back to sleep.
  • I feel physically exhausted, but my mind is racing.
  • I rely on my phone or the television to distract myself until I fall asleep.
  • I wake up feeling unrefreshed, regardless of how many hours I was in bed.

Practical Self-Help Guide

Here are five evidence-informed strategies to help you improve the quality of your rest.

Tip 1

Create a Buffer Zone

Your brain needs time to decelerate before sleep. Transitioning straight from work or screens to bed rarely works.

Try this today

Designate the 45 minutes before bed as a "buffer zone." Read a physical book, stretch, or listen to soft music.

Tip 2

The 20-Minute Rule

Laying in bed awake for long periods teaches your brain to associate your bed with frustration and wakefulness, rather than sleep.

Try this today

If you cannot sleep after 20 minutes, get up and sit in a dim room doing something boring until you feel sleepy. Then return to bed.

Tip 3

Control Your Light Exposure

Your circadian rhythm is governed by light. Blue light in the evening suppresses melatonin, the hormone that makes you sleepy.

Try this today

Turn down the brightness on all screens 2 hours before bed, and use warm, low-level lighting in your home.

Tip 4

Keep a Consistent Wake Time

Sleeping in on weekends disrupts your biological clock, making it harder to fall asleep on Sunday night.

Try this today

Set your alarm for the exact same time every day, seven days a week, regardless of how badly you slept the night before.

Tip 5

Cool Down the Room

Your core body temperature needs to drop slightly for you to initiate and maintain deep sleep.

Try this today

Keep your bedroom slightly cooler than the rest of the house (around 18°C or 65°F).

Today's Challenge

Tonight, leave your phone charging in another room and use a traditional alarm clock instead.

When to Seek Professional Support

Self-help is a wonderful starting point, but it does not replace professional therapy. If your sleep issues persist and interfere with your daily life, work, or relationships, please reach out to a licensed professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I really need 8 hours of sleep?

Not necessarily. Sleep needs vary between 7 and 9 hours for most adults. Quality is just as important as quantity. Focus on how you feel the next day rather than a specific number.

Is it okay to take naps?

Short power naps (20-30 minutes) early in the afternoon can be beneficial. However, long naps or napping late in the day will reduce your sleep drive at night.

What if I wake up at 3 AM?

This is very common. Do not check the time, as this triggers anxiety. Use deep breathing, and if you cannot get back to sleep after 20 minutes, get out of bed and read under dim light.

How to Stop Overthinking

A practical, evidence-informed guide to breaking the cycle of rumination, silencing your inner critic, and quieting your mind before sleep.

Why Does This Happen?

Overthinking is incredibly common. It's simply your brain trying to protect you by anticipating problems before they occur.

When you feel stressed or uncertain, your mind begins to treat every thought as an urgent threat that must be solved immediately. Instead of finding a solution, you end up caught in a loop of 'what-ifs,' replaying past conversations or imagining future worst-case scenarios. It's exhausting, but it's a habit you can gently unlearn.

Signs You Might Relate

  • I repeatedly replay past conversations in my head.
  • I struggle to fall asleep because my mind races.
  • I constantly imagine worst-case scenarios.
  • I frequently seek reassurance from others to make sure I didn't make a mistake.
  • I find it difficult to make small decisions out of fear of making the wrong choice.

Practical Self-Help Guide

Here are five evidence-informed strategies you can use to break the overthinking loop.

Tip 1

The Brain Dump

When your mind is full, your brain holds onto thoughts tightly so you won't forget them. Writing them down signals to your brain that they are safely stored elsewhere.

Try this today

Write every thought on paper before bed for five minutes. Do not filter or organize them—just write.

Tip 2

Challenge the Thought

Thoughts are not facts, but overthinking treats them as absolute truth. By questioning the thought, you can distance yourself from it.

Try this today

When a stressful thought arises, ask yourself: Is this a fact, a fear, or just a possibility?

Tip 3

Set a Worry Time

Telling yourself "don't worry" rarely works. Giving yourself permission to worry at a specific time allows you to postpone anxiety rather than fight it.

Try this today

Give yourself 15 minutes each day (e.g., at 4:00 PM) to think about your worries. If a worry pops up earlier, tell yourself you will deal with it during "worry time."

Tip 4

Practice Box Breathing

Overthinking triggers your body's stress response. Slowing your breath physically signals to your nervous system that you are safe.

Try this today

Breathe in for 4 seconds. Hold for 4. Breathe out for 4. Hold for 4. Repeat this five times.

Tip 5

Shift Your Focus to the Present

Rumination lives in the past, and worry lives in the future. Grounding yourself in the present moment interrupts the cognitive loop.

Try this today

Name 3 things you can see right now, 2 things you can hear, and 1 thing you can touch.

Today's Challenge

No phone for the first 30 minutes after waking.

When to Seek Professional Support

Self-help is a wonderful starting point, but it does not replace professional therapy. If your overthinking patterns persist and interfere with your daily life, work, or relationships, please reach out to a licensed professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is overthinking a mental illness?

No, overthinking itself is not a mental illness. It is a very common thought pattern. However, chronic overthinking can be a symptom of anxiety disorders.

How do I stop overthinking at night?

Practicing a "brain dump" by writing down all your worries before getting into bed is one of the most effective ways to signal to your brain that it is safe to rest.

Can overthinking cause physical symptoms?

Yes. The stress caused by overthinking triggers cortisol and adrenaline release, which can cause muscle tension, headaches, and fatigue.

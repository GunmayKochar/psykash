Navigating Social Media Comparison

Protect your self-worth online by understanding the psychology behind the highlight reel.

Why Does This Happen?

Social media algorithms are designed to show you the most engaging, visually stunning, and extreme versions of human existence. When you scroll, your brain is not seeing reality; it is seeing the top 1% of everyone else's lives.

Your brain, however, processes these images as if they were the normal baseline. When you compare your mundane, unfiltered Tuesday morning to someone else's heavily edited holiday photos, the resulting gap creates intense feelings of inadequacy and depression.

Signs You Might Relate

  • I spend more time trying to take the perfect photo of an event than actually enjoying it.
  • I delete posts if they don't get enough likes within the first hour.
  • I feel like everyone else has their career and relationships figured out, except for me.
  • I secretly feel happy when someone I envy online posts about a failure or struggle.
  • I feel a wave of anxiety immediately after closing Instagram or TikTok.

Practical Self-Help Guide

Here are five evidence-informed strategies to protect your mental health online.

Tip 1

Mute Freely

You do not have to unfollow people (which might cause social friction) to stop seeing their content. Muting is a silent boundary.

Try this today

Mute three accounts today that consistently make you feel envious or "less than."

Tip 2

Remember the "Outtakes"

Behind every perfect photo are fifty outtakes, perfect lighting, and often, a significant amount of stress to capture it.

Try this today

Next time you envy a post, explicitly remind yourself: "This is a staged performance, not a documentary."

Tip 3

Focus on Creation over Consumption

Passive scrolling leads to depression. Using the internet to create, connect, or learn is correlated with positive well-being.

Try this today

Limit your time scrolling the feed. Use that time instead to message a friend directly or read a long-form article.

Tip 4

Delay Posting

Posting in real-time pulls you out of the present moment and ties your enjoyment of an event to the digital validation you receive.

Try this today

Take photos if you want, but wait until you get home to post them. Stay in the moment.

Tip 5

Audit Your Emotional State

Most people scroll mindlessly without noticing how it actually makes them feel physically and emotionally.

Try this today

Before opening an app, rate your mood out of 10. After 15 minutes of scrolling, rate it again. Notice the trend.

Today's Challenge

Go onto your most-used social platform and intentionally follow three accounts that focus on nature, art, or comedy rather than lifestyle.

"The reason we struggle with insecurity is because we compare our behind-the-scenes with everyone else's highlight reel."

When to Seek Professional Support

Self-help is a wonderful starting point, but it does not replace professional therapy. If your social media anxiety persist and interfere with your daily life, work, or relationships, please reach out to a licensed professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it better to just quit completely?

For some, yes. But for others, social media is a vital tool for staying in touch. You do not have to quit; you just have to curate it ruthlessly.

Why do I care about likes from strangers?

Likes trigger dopamine, mimicking social approval. It is a biological hack that makes you care about the opinions of people you don't even know.

How do I explain to a friend that I muted them?

You rarely have to explain, as they are not notified. If asked, say: "I am taking a step back from looking at feeds right now for my mental health, but I still want to talk to you directly!"
